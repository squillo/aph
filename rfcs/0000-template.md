## The problem

What breaks, misleads, or cannot be expressed today, with the spec section
cited. This section exists because a change without a named problem is a
preference, and the record of the problem outlives the fix.

## The design

What changes, concretely enough to disagree with. Wire examples where the
wire moves.

## Alternatives considered

Named, with why each was rejected. The rejected options are half the value
of an RFC — they are what stops the next person from proposing them cold.

## Compatibility

What happens to an artifact minted yesterday, and to a verifier that has not
updated. Say "breaking" plainly if it is breaking, and state the version
consequence under CONTRIBUTING.md's rules.

## Security considerations

Which threat in the security-considerations document this interacts with, or
which exclusion it moves. "None found" is an answer; silence is not.

## What this deliberately does not do

The scope line, drawn on purpose, so the next reader knows the omissions are
decisions rather than oversights.
